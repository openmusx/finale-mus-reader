    Twenty-one text blocks and three expressions, each holding one Text Tool
    insert or style command and nothing else, so that every one is identified by
    its own block number. Written to decode the binary Enigma command codes,
    which Finale stores in the MUS from Finale 2006 and which no public source
    describes.

    Finale 2006 is the only release that can answer this. It is the first to write
    those codes and the last to export ETF, so the same document exists both
    encoded and spelled out: the .mus stores `^\x8c\x01\x01\x01\x02` where the
    .etf writes `^date(1)`. The Finale 27 companion agrees with the ETF on all 24
    records, giving each pairing two independent witnesses. Together they fix 25
    command codes and three things about the argument encoding that no other
    document could settle:

      * it is one value, not a sequence of shorter ones. Block 6 spells a page
        offset of 275 as `01 01 01 01 01 02 02 04`; read as two arguments the
        same bytes are 0 and 275, so a zero offset could never have told them
        apart.
      * the digit range runs the full 0 to 15. Block 21's `^superscript(15)` is
        `01 01 01 01 01 01 01 10`, the only place a nibble of 0xf appears
        anywhere, which is what confirms digits are stored one greater than
        their value across the whole range rather than only where observed.
      * an argument is signed. Block 19's `^baseline(-13)` is
        `10 10 10 10 10 10 10 04`, or 0xfffffff3; read unsigned it is
        4294967283.

    The document also establishes that Finale 2006's File Info still has exactly
    four fields, title, composer, copyright and description, so the lyricist,
    arranger and subtitle that musxdom's FileInfoText models arrived later.
